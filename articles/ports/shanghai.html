Shanghai in a Port Day: The Bund, Yu Garden, and the Skyline

November 21, 2026 · By Hedgewiz · Port guide

Shanghai is the biggest prize on a China cruise — and the hardest port to do well in one day, because the terminal most large ships use sits a long way from the city. Get the transfer right and the day works beautifully: a free waterfront promenade, a 400-year-old garden, and a skyline that looks like the future.

Arriving: two terminals, very different days

Most large ships dock at the Wusongkou International Cruise Terminal in Baoshan District, at No. 1 Baoyang Road, where the Huangpu meets the Yangtze — roughly 25 to 30 kilometres north of the Bund, with no metro station of its own. A taxi or DiDi into the centre takes 45 to 70 minutes and costs roughly ¥130–200 each way. The cheap route is the terminal shuttle to Baoyang Road station, then Metro Line 3 into town, for about ¥5–7 — but it can take up to two hours each way, so it only makes sense if the ship is in overnight. Smaller ships use the Shanghai Port International Cruise Terminal on the North Bund, about 2 kilometres from the Bund itself — a 10-minute taxi for roughly ¥20–30, or a short ride on Metro Line 12 from the station named International Cruise Terminal. Check your ship's documents before sailing: the difference is about an hour and a half each way. Neither terminal tenders; you walk off down the gangway. Immigration and customs on turnaround days can be slow — allow time for the queue.

Sort the visa rules for your nationality before the cruise — cruise passengers are treated differently from air arrivals, and the rules change. Carry your passport ashore.

The Bund first

Start at the Bund, the waterfront promenade on the west bank of the Huangpu. It is free, it is the single best thing in Shanghai, and it is at its best in the late afternoon, when the colonial-era facades catch the light and, after dark, the towers of Pudong light up across the river. If you want to go up a tower, the Shanghai Tower's observation deck on the 118th floor is the highest public viewpoint in China at 546 metres, roughly ¥180 for an adult — pick a clear day only, since haze wipes out the view. The ¥2 public ferry across the Huangpu puts you right under the towers.

Yu Garden and the Old City

From the Bund, it is a short taxi or metro ride to Yu Garden, the Ming-dynasty classical garden in the Old City — pavilions, rockeries and koi ponds behind a wall, with the free-to-wander Yuyuan Bazaar around it. Entry is roughly ¥30–40 depending on the season, and the garden is closed on Mondays, so check the day before building the plan around it. Go in the morning, before the tour groups fill the bazaar: an hour inside the garden is enough, then eat there — soup dumplings and scallion pancakes from the street stalls run roughly ¥20–40 for a filling lunch. The surrounding Old City streets, around the City God Temple, are worth a slow wander rather than a photo stop.

Nanjing Road and the afternoon

Nanjing Road, the pedestrian shopping street, runs from People's Square to the Bund — walk it west to east in the afternoon and you end up back at the Bund for the lights. It is a shopping street, not a sight in itself, so do not give it more than an hour unless shopping is the point. If you have time left, the French Concession's plane-tree streets around Wukang Road are a quieter contrast, about 20 minutes by metro from People's Square.

Getting around

The metro is clean, cheap and signed in English, at roughly ¥3–9 per ride — but from Wusongkou it is the slow option, so use it inside the city, not to get there. Taxis are metered and cheap by Western standards; few drivers speak English, so have your destination written in Chinese characters, or use DiDi's English app. Shanghai is close to cashless: set up Alipay or WeChat Pay with a foreign card before sailing, and carry some cash anyway for the smallest stalls.

What a port day costs (approximate, per person)

  • Taxi from Wusongkou to the Bund and back (split two ways): roughly ¥130–200 each
  • Metro rides inside the city: roughly ¥10–20
  • Yu Garden: roughly ¥30–40
  • Shanghai Tower observation deck: roughly ¥180
  • Street-food lunch in the Yuyuan Bazaar: roughly ¥20–40
  • The Bund, the ferry across the river, Nanjing Road: free or about ¥2
  • Comfortable total without the tower: roughly ¥200–300

Tips for cruisers

  • Budget the return like a flight. From Wusongkou, leave the city at least two hours before all-aboard: traffic, the 25-kilometre drive and re-entry formalities all stack up. From the North Bund terminal, an hour is enough.
  • Do not build the day around Yu Garden on a Monday. It is closed.
  • Stay for the lights if the ship allows it. The Bund after dark is the postcard — but never cut the all-aboard time close for it.

Prices and hours are approximate and were checked in November 2026; fares and ticket prices change, and the taxi fare from Wusongkou varies with traffic. Confirm on the day if a price decides the plan.
