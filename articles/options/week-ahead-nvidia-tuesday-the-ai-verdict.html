Week Ahead: Nvidia Tuesday — the AI Verdict, the Consumer Parade, and a Tape Sitting Just Below Its Gamma Flip

November 15, 2026 · By Hedgewiz · Originally published on Substack

By Hedgewiz — for OptionsWizard members. Week of Nov 16–20, 2026. All levels from Friday Nov 13 closes and Friday's options book, drafted Sunday Nov 15, 2026 (Los Angeles). Market insights only.

Last week was the bounce, not the repair. The S&P 500 rose 2.6% to 754.37 and the Nasdaq 3.6% to 679.89, the VIX fell 25% to 19.30, and chips bounced 9.4% — but SPY still sits below its 50-day average, QQQ is still below its 200-day, and only 42.8% of stocks sit above their 50-day. Then Friday delivered the week's real story after the close: Nvidia's Jensen Huang resigned, and the stock — already down 12.8% on the week to $162.85, 31% off its peak — reports Tuesday after the close with an interim CEO, a $108B revenue guide, and a ±9.6% expected move. The same week brings the fullest consumer read before Black Friday (Home Depot and Disney Tuesday, Target/Lowe's/TJX Wednesday, Walmart Thursday), October retail sales Tuesday morning, and the October FOMC minutes Wednesday afternoon. This is a two-handed week: one hand holds the AI story, the other holds the consumer. Anthropic's IPO — pricing Wednesday evening, trading Thursday at a ~$1.4T valuation — is the most important IPO for a pure AI play, and the week's third live event.

Neutral into Tuesday, and honest about why: the bounce repaired the damage of the prior week without repairing the tape — SPY reclaimed its 200-day (731.92) but not its 50-day (764.43), QQQ is 0.3% below its 200-day (682.24), breadth is still narrow, and the Fear & Greed index reads 39 (Fear), up from 22. But the options book is hedged, not panicked — the big put walls sit below spot and held through the prior week's flush — and Friday's tape showed buyers still step in on good numbers (Liftoff +30.6%, Trade Desk +21.3% on the week). The read: let Nvidia and retail sales arrive before deciding what the bounce means. A clean Nvidia earnings release with the market holding the 750 SPY put wall is a rally setup into Friday's monthly expiry; a miss that breaks 730 turns the same book into a fast tape down.

Earnings this week

What to watch is not the quarter, it is the guide and the voice delivering it: Jensen Huang resigned Nov 12, CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, and this is the first earnings release under new leadership. Past earnings releases say a beat is not enough — Nvidia has beaten its own revenue guide 10 straight times and still fell after 6 of those 10: Nov 2025 −3.2%, Feb 2026 −5.5%, May 2026 −1.8%, then Aug 2026 +8.7% when the guide did the work. The bounce was broadest where last week's damage was worst, and energy led for a second week; rate-sensitive and China-linked names lagged while the 10-year yield rose to 5.15%.

The options book

The two expiries tell the same story, which is itself information — and SOXX's Nov 27 max pain is pulled above spot by split-adjusted legacy call strikes (the old 490 is now 163.33).

The tape sits just below its flip on all four indexes: dealers are positioned in a way that tends to amplify moves below spot rather than damp them — selling into weakness and buying into strength — until price reclaims the flip. There is no clean positive-gamma pin this week; the max-pain levels are where the book balances, not where it protects.

The monthly SPY put/call ratio is tail-hedge noise — the book is dominated by deep strikes like the 500 put (319,566 contracts) and the 510 put (200,934), against a 158,785-contract call wall at 800. Those are not bets that SPY goes to 500; they are crash insurance, and they stay on through every expiry. The weekly ratio is the honest one. QQQ is the cleanest book of the four, IWM the most hedged. NVDA's 0.70 looks call-heavy until you see where the calls sit (210 and 230, from when the stock lived there). A put/call ratio alone lies — always ask where the open interest lives before concluding what it means.

3. NVDA bull put spread into the earnings release — Nov 20 150/145 put spread, about $1.12 credit (sell the 150 put at 2.92, buy the 145 put at 1.80). Width $5, max loss $3.88, breakeven $148.88. Note the 150 put showed a crossed quote in Friday's book (bid 2.95 / ask 2.89), so treat the credit as approximate. If it gaps below $148.88, the trade is wrong and the defined loss is the cost of being wrong — keep it small, because a 9.6% expected move cuts both ways.

And the standing lesson from last week, worth repeating because it just paid out in reverse: earnings gaps do not respect stop losses. GOOGL's −12.6% session three weeks ago and the AI unwind that took Nvidia 31% off its peak are what "defined risk" is for. Naked premium into a known event is picking up pennies in front of a steamroller that has a schedule — and never write options right after an earnings release, when the premium has already crashed.

This week is three verdicts in one: Tuesday night is the AI verdict — Nvidia's first earnings release under an interim CEO, with a $108B guide and a 31% slide behind it; Tuesday through Thursday is the consumer verdict, from retail sales to Walmart; and Thursday is the IPO verdict — Anthropic's first day of trading, the most important IPO for a pure AI play, at a ~$1.4T valuation, after pricing Wednesday evening. The options book says hedged, pinned just below its gamma flip, and short gamma below spot — a tape that can move fast in either direction once Nvidia or a single retail earnings release breaks the pin. Position for the crush after the earnings releases, keep the risk defined, and let the week's information arrive before deciding what it means.
