Week Ahead: CPI Tuesday, PPI Friday — and Four AI Names Have to Prove the Spending

November 8, 2026 · By Hedgewiz · Originally published on Substack

Last week was the second leg of the AI-spending reckoning, and this week decides whether it was a flush or the start of something longer. The S&P 500 fell 2.7% to 735.29, the Nasdaq fell 5.0% to 656.10, the VIX jumped 32% to 25.64, and chips were the worst of it — SOXX fell 13.1% to 136.53 (after a 3-for-1 split on Nov 5) as the semiconductor complex gave back its autumn. Friday bounced on a strong jobs report (+165,000 payrolls vs. 50,000–80,000 expected), but the bounce did not repair the damage: only 38.9% of stocks sit above their 50-day average. Into that tape walks the week's real test — October CPI Tuesday morning, October PPI Friday morning, and four AI-infrastructure earnings reports (Take-Two Monday night, CoreWeave Tuesday night, Nebius, Cisco and Applied Materials Thursday) that have to justify the capex bill the market just started questioning. This is an inflation week with an earnings spine, not the other way round.

Neutral-to-cautious into Tuesday, and honest about why: the indexes are sitting on or below their 200-day averages (QQQ 656.10 vs. a 680.89 200-day; IWM 273.67 vs. 279.00; SPY 735.29 barely above its 730.38 200-day), breadth is narrow, and the Fear & Greed index reads 22 — Extreme Fear. But the options book is hedged hard below spot, not panicked above it, and Friday's jobs bounce says buyers are still willing to step in on good news. The read: let CPI arrive before deciding what the bounce means. A soft CPI with the market holding the 730–745 SPY put cluster is a rally setup into Friday; a hot CPI that breaks 730 turns the same book into a fast tape down.

One name, studied properly: AppLovin (APP)

What was observed. AppLovin reported Q3 on Nov 4 and the stock did what great earnings reports do in a bad tape: it rose 31% on the earnings day while the Nasdaq fell more than 5%, then kept going to +71.2% on the week — the single biggest quality move in the market — while still sitting 47% below its $738 high. The move was not a short squeeze (APP is not on the squeeze list) and not a sector move (its nearest peer, The Trade Desk, reports Monday night and has been falling). It was the quarter.

The evidence. Revenue $2.185B, +55% year over year, roughly $100M above the top end of management's own guidance. Adjusted EBITDA $1.842B, +59%, at an 84% margin — the margin widened at scale, which is the hard trick. Net income +72% to $1.439B; diluted EPS $4.30 against ~$4.04 expected. Q4 guidance is $2.365–2.395B revenue (43–44% growth) with adjusted EBITDA of $1.995–2.025B — above the ~$2.25B consensus. Gaming revenue grew nearly 50% on a partial rollout of the new Axon model architecture. Three independent checks line up: the earnings report itself, the guidance raise, and the stock's refusal to give any of it back in a week when the Nasdaq fell 5%.

The moat. Axon is an AI recommendation engine that matches advertisers with audiences inside apps and games and charges on measurable return — the more advertisers spend, the better the model gets, the more advertisers spend. Management narrowed the company to a pure-play software model (apps portfolio divested), launched self-serve globally in June, and is now pushing into connected TV and e-commerce beyond its gaming base. An 84% EBITDA margin at an $8.7B annualized revenue run rate is not a story stock — it is a toll booth.

Valuation. Expensive on any trailing measure after a 71% week, and the stock still trades below its 200-day average ($409.30) — the market has not yet decided this is a new high. That is why this is a watchlist name, not a chase: the entry is the pullback, not the earnings report.

Risks. The next-generation Axon model is only partially rolled out and management would not commit to a full-deployment timeline; if model improvements stall, price growth slows and revenue misses guidance the way Q2 did. Securities litigation and regulatory scrutiny are live. E-commerce is unproven at scale, and The Trade Desk's earnings report Monday night is the first peer check — a weak TTD could re-rate the whole ad-tech group regardless of APP's quarter.

What would change the view. A Q4 miss against the $2.365–2.395B guide in February, a stalled model rollout, or the stock losing $330–350 on a pullback and not reclaiming it. Trigger: a pullback toward $330–350 that holds, or a close above the 200-day near $409. Until one of those happens, it is the best idea on the board — and it stays on the watchlist.

The options book

OI walls, front week (Nov 13) vs monthly (Nov 20). The walls barely move between the two expiries, which is itself information: SPY max pain 755 → 760, QQQ 690 → 705, IWM 278 → 280. The monthly book is much bigger and much more tail-hedged — SPY Nov 20 put open interest is 4.07x calls, dominated by deep strikes like the 500 put (319,759 contracts) and the 510 put (200,757), against a 160,089-contract call wall at 800. Those are not bets that SPY goes to 500. They are crash insurance, and they stay on through every expiry. The actionable hedging is always the near-money cluster: SPY 730–745, QQQ 650–685, IWM 265–276, SOXX 125–138.

Gamma flip — read as a regime, not a single line. On Friday's book, front-week net gamma is negative at spot for SPY, QQQ and IWM: put gamma concentrated at SPY 730–745, QQQ 650 and IWM 272–276 outweighs call gamma near spot. In plain terms, dealers are positioned in a way that tends to amplify moves below spot rather than damp them — selling into weakness and buying into strength — until price reclaims the call walls above (SPY 776–780, QQQ 700, IWM 285). There is no clean positive-gamma pin this week; the max-pain levels (SPY 755, QQQ 690, IWM 278) are where the book balances, not where it protects. SOXX is the exception in the other direction: its max pain sits far above spot, which says the book wants chips higher — but the tape just said otherwise, and the tape wins.

Put/call by expiry. SPY: 1.34 (Nov 13), 4.07 (Nov 20, tail-hedge dominated). QQQ: 1.16 (Nov 13), 1.06 (Nov 20) — the cleanest book of the four. IWM: 1.73 (Nov 13), 3.08 (Nov 20) — the most hedged. SOXX: 1.13 (Nov 13), 1.73 (Nov 20). TTWO's front-week book is the most lopsided of the earnings names at 3.99 — the market is buying downside protection into the earnings report, not upside. Lesson from last week, repeated: a put/call ratio alone lies. SPY's monthly 4.07 sounds apocalyptic until you see where the puts sit. Always ask where the open interest lives before concluding what it means.

No positions, no trades staged — these are the levels that would make each name interesting, and the trigger that would put it on the table. The standing setup is unchanged: the board is watch-only, and the working idea has been to wait for November strength and short into it, not into a flush — last week's flush arrived early and without the run-up, and it has not changed the board's logic, only its prices.

A broad software bounce that fails at those zones is more informative than any single name.

And the standing lesson from last week, worth repeating because it just paid out in reverse: earnings gaps do not respect stop losses. GOOGL's −12.6% session two weeks ago and NOW's −33.6% week are what "defined risk" is for. Naked premium into a known event is picking up pennies in front of a steamroller that has a schedule — and never write options right after an earnings report, when the premium has already crashed.

This week is two trades in one: Monday through Thursday is an AI-spending verdict led by Take-Two, CoreWeave, Cisco and Applied Materials, and Tuesday and Friday are inflation reports that decide whether the Fed hikes in December. The options book says hedged, pinned, and short gamma below spot — a tape that can move fast in either direction once CPI or a single earnings report breaks the pin. Position for the crush after the earnings reports, keep the risk defined, and let the week's information arrive before deciding what it means.
