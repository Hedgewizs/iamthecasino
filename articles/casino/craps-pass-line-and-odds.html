The Pass Line and Odds: The Only Bet With No House Edge

November 21, 2026 · By Hedgewiz · Casino

A craps table looks chaotic — chips everywhere, players shouting, a dozen bets on the felt. But almost all of the smart play comes down to two bets: the pass line, and the odds bet that goes behind it. The odds bet is the only wager in the casino with no house edge at all. That sounds like a loophole, and in a narrow sense it is. But it only helps if you understand exactly what it does — and what it does not do.

How the pass line works

The pass line is the basic bet in craps, and the one nearly everyone at the table is making. On the first roll of a round — the come-out roll — a 7 or 11 wins even money, while a 2, 3, or 12 loses. Any other number — 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, or 10 — becomes the point. The shooter keeps rolling: if the point repeats before a 7 appears, the pass line wins even money; if a 7 appears first, "seven out," the pass line loses and the round ends.

The pass line is a good bet by casino standards, but not a fair one. Its house edge is 1.41%: for every $100 you put on the pass line and let resolve, you lose about $1.41 on average. The edge comes from the come-out roll, where the instant losers slightly outweigh the instant winners, and from the point phase, where the 7 is the most likely number on any roll. The math is fixed. No dice-setting or table selection changes it.

What the odds bet is

Once a point is established, you can make a second bet, called taking the odds. You place it behind your pass line bet, and it wins or loses with the same outcome: it pays if the point repeats before a 7, and loses on seven-out. The difference is the payout. Instead of even money, the odds bet pays at true odds — the payout that exactly matches the real probability of the point repeating before a 7.

True odds depend on the point. A point of 4 or 10 pays 2 to 1: three ways to roll a 4 against six ways to roll a 7. A point of 5 or 9 pays 3 to 2, and a point of 6 or 8 pays 6 to 5 — five ways against six. In each case the payout is exactly fair: a $10 odds bet on a point of 6 stands to win $12, which is precisely what the probabilities justify. No edge is skimmed off, which is why the odds bet has a house edge of zero. Casinos cap the odds, usually at a multiple of your pass line bet, because it is a bet they cannot beat.

Why "no house edge" does not mean free money

The odds bet has no edge, but it also has no advantage. Its expected value is exactly zero: over the long run, you expect to win exactly as much on the odds as you lose. It does not cancel the edge on your pass line bet, and it does not turn craps into a winning game. The $1.41 you expect to lose per $100 on the pass line is still lost, dollar for dollar, no matter how much odds you take.

What odds change is the edge as a percentage of your total action. Take a $10 pass line bet with $30 of odds behind it. Your expected loss is still about 14 cents — the 1.41% applies only to the $10 pass line portion, and the $30 of odds expects to break even. But you now have $40 at risk instead of $10, so your expected loss is 0.35% of your total money in play rather than 1.41%. Taking full odds makes your overall play the lowest-edge proposition in the casino. That is a statement about the percentage, not the dollars — and the swings get bigger both ways.

What this means in practice

If you are going to play craps at all, the pass line with full odds is the way to do it. The centre of the table is full of bets with edges of 9%, 11%, even 16% — the hardways, the field, the proposition bets in the middle. But size the odds to your bankroll, not to the table maximum: 2x or 3x odds cuts your effective edge well below 1% without turning every roll into a bankroll event. And remember the two bets have different rules after the point is set — your pass line bet is locked in, while your odds bet can be reduced or removed at any time.

Keep the frame straight. The odds bet is the casino's one honest wager, and the house still wins in the long run, because the pass line bet that unlocks it is not honest. The odds bet lets you dilute the edge on your total action to a fraction of a percent. It does not let you beat the game.
