Banker vs Player: Why Banker Wins Slightly More

November 21, 2026 · By Hedgewiz · Casino

Baccarat is the simplest game on the casino floor, and one of the most misunderstood. You make a single choice — banker, player, or tie — and then you watch. You make no decisions during the hand and cannot misplay anything. Yet one of the three bets is measurably better than the others. The reason is a small quirk in the drawing rules that gives the banker hand a slight edge — and the casino takes that edge back from you with a 5% commission.

The card rules in plain words

Two hands are dealt: one called player, one called banker. The names are just labels — you can bet on either hand, and "player" does not mean you. Each hand gets two cards, and the hand closer to 9 wins. Cards 2 through 9 are worth face value, 10s and face cards are worth zero, and aces are worth 1. Totals above 9 drop the tens digit: a 9 and a 6 total 15, which counts as 5. A hand of 8 or 9 on the first two cards is a natural, and the hand ends immediately unless both sides have one.

If neither side has a natural, a third card may be drawn, and the rules are fixed — the dealer follows them, and no one at the table has any say. The player hand goes first: it stands on 6 or 7 and draws on 0 through 5. The banker hand then acts: it stands on 7, draws on 0, 1, and 2, and on 3 through 6 its decision depends on what third card the player drew. You do not need to memorise that table — the dealer handles it — but one feature of it is the whole story: the banker hand draws after seeing the player's third card. It acts with more information, and that is the source of its advantage.

Why banker wins more often

Because the banker hand moves second, it wins more hands. Over a standard eight-deck shoe, banker wins about 45.86% of hands, player about 44.62%, and about 9.52% are ties. Strip out the ties, and banker wins just over half of the decisive hands — about 50.68%. The gap is small, roughly one extra win in a hundred hands, but it never goes away: it is baked into the drawing rules. No pattern of past results changes the odds of the next hand.

The 5% commission math

If banker wins more often and paid even money, betting on banker would be a winning proposition — the casino would lose money on it. So it does not pay even money. A winning banker bet pays 19 to 20: stake $20, collect $19 of profit, and the house keeps $1 as commission. At most tables the commission is collected when you leave or in small amounts as you go, but the effect is the same: 5% of your banker winnings go to the house.

Run the arithmetic on $1 bets. The banker hand wins about 45.86% of hands, the player hand wins about 44.62%, and about 9.52% tie and push — your stake comes back. On a banker bet: 45.86% × $0.95 − 44.62% = about −$0.0106 per dollar, a house edge of 1.06%. On a player bet, which pays even money with no commission: 44.62% − 45.86% = about −$0.0124, an edge of 1.24%. The commission slightly over-corrects the banker's advantage, leaving banker the better bet by a whisker.

The tie bet usually pays 8 to 1, which sounds generous until you do the same arithmetic: ties happen only about 9.52% of the time, so the payout would need to be much higher to be fair. The house edge on the tie bet is 14.36% — more than ten times the edge on banker. It is one of the worst bets on the casino floor.

What to do with this

The practical conclusion is short: bet banker. It is the best bet at the baccarat table, and one of the best bets in the entire casino — a 1.06% edge is lower than the pass line in craps, lower than most blackjack games played by feel, and far lower than any slot machine or roulette wheel. Bet player if you prefer it: the difference costs about 18 cents per $100 wagered. Never bet the tie.

Two cautions. First, the 5% commission is usually collected in a lump when you leave, which makes it easy to forget: some of any win is already spoken for. Second, a hand takes under a minute, so 60 or 80 hands an hour is easy. At $25 a hand on banker, that is $1,500 to $2,000 wagered per hour — an expected loss of roughly $16 to $21 an hour, before variance. Set a session limit before you sit down.

Baccarat rewards exactly one skill: picking the best bet and leaving the rest alone. Banker wins slightly more because it moves second; the commission takes that back, plus a little. What is left is the cheapest seat in the house — as long as you remember it is still the house's seat.
