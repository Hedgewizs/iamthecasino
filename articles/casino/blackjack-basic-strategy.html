Basic Strategy in Plain Words

November 21, 2026 · By Hedgewiz · Casino

Blackjack is the only game in the casino where your decisions change the house edge. At roulette or baccarat, you pick a bet and watch. At blackjack, every hand asks you a question — hit, stand, double, split — and the answer determines how much the game costs you. Most players answer by feel, which costs roughly 2% per dollar wagered. Basic strategy is the set of answers worked out by computer simulation: for every hand against every dealer upcard, it gives the play with the highest expected value. Follow it, and the house edge falls to roughly 0.5% under good rules.

Basic strategy is usually shown as a chart — a grid of coloured squares that looks like homework. It does not have to be learned that way. The whole thing reduces to a handful of rules. The logic is always the same: the dealer's upcard tells you how likely the dealer is to bust, and your hand tells you how much risk a hit carries.

Hard hands: the dealer's card decides

A hard hand is one with no ace, or one where the ace must count as 1 — a total that can bust if you hit it. Always stand on hard 17 or higher: the dealer must hit 16 and stand on 17. When the dealer shows 2 through 6 — the bust cards — stand on 13 through 16 and let the dealer do the busting. When the dealer shows 7 through ace, hit 12 through 16: standing on a stiff total is just waiting to lose. Hard 12 is the exception: stand against 4, 5, or 6, but hit against 2 or 3.

Doubling is where the money is made: put up a second bet equal to your first, take exactly one more card, and stand. Always double 11 against anything except a dealer ace, double 10 against a dealer 2 through 9, and double 9 against a dealer 3 through 6 — when your hand is likely to improve and the dealer is likely to be weak.

Soft hands: the ace gives you a free shot

A soft hand holds an ace counted as 11 — soft 18 is ace plus 7. The ace is flexible: if you hit and the total would go over 21, the ace drops to 1 and you are still alive. You cannot bust a soft hand with one card, so play them aggressively. Soft 18 is the headline case: stand against a dealer 2, 7, or 8; double against 3 through 6; hit against 9, 10, or ace. Soft 17 and below: hit, or double against 3 through 6.

Pairs: split the good ones, keep the bad ones together

When you are dealt a pair, you can split it into two hands for a second bet. Always split aces and 8s. A pair of 8s is a hard 16, the worst hand in blackjack; splitting gives you two hands starting on 8. Never split 5s or 10s: a pair of 5s is a hard 10, and a pair of 10s is 20. Split 2s, 3s, and 7s against a dealer 2 through 7; 6s against 2 through 6; 9s against 2 through 6 or 8 or 9, never 7; 4s only against 5 or 6, and only if doubling after a split is allowed.

Why it cuts the edge to roughly 0.5%

Every play in basic strategy was settled by simulating millions of hands and keeping the option with the best average result. The gains come from three places: standing on stiff hands when the dealer is likely to bust, doubling when you are the favourite, and splitting pairs worth more apart than together. It promises the best average over thousands of hands: a house edge of roughly half a percent, under good rules — blackjack pays 3 to 2, the dealer stands on soft 17, and you can double after splitting.

The rules matter as much as the strategy. The single biggest trap in modern blackjack is the 6-to-5 table, where a blackjack pays $6 for every $5 bet instead of $3 for every $2. That change alone adds about 1.4% to the house edge — nearly tripling the cost of the game — and it swamps everything basic strategy saves you. Check the felt before you sit down: if it says "Blackjack pays 6 to 5," find another table.

Never take insurance. The insurance side bet pays 2 to 1, but the dealer holds a ten only about 30% of the time, so it carries an edge of roughly 7% or more. "Even money" on your own blackjack is the same bet in disguise — decline that too. Half a percent is a long-run average; in the short run, variance dominates — you can play perfectly for an hour and still lose. The only skill that has ever turned blackjack into a winning game is card counting, which bets bigger when the remaining deck is rich in tens and aces.

Stand on 17, let the dealer bust, double strong hands against weak cards, split aces and 8s, never take insurance, never sit at a 6-to-5 table. That is basic strategy in plain words.
