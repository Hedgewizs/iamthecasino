European vs American Roulette: One Pocket Doubles the Edge

November 21, 2026 · By Hedgewiz · Casino

Walk into a casino and you will usually find two kinds of roulette wheel, and they look almost identical. One has 37 pockets, the other has 38. The difference is a single green pocket — a double zero — and it is the most expensive pocket in the building. On the 37-pocket wheel, the house edge is 2.70%. On the 38-pocket wheel, it is 5.26%. One extra pocket nearly doubles what the game costs you. If you play roulette at all, knowing which wheel you are standing at is the single most important decision you will make.

The two wheels

The European wheel has 37 pockets: the numbers 1 through 36, plus a single green zero. The American wheel has 38 pockets: the same 1 through 36, plus a green zero and a green double zero. The betting layout is the same on both, and so are the payouts — a single number pays 35 to 1, a red-or-black bet pays even money, a dozen pays 2 to 1. Only the number of pockets — and the odds — differ. That sameness is the trap: you are paid as if there were 36 pockets on the wheel, while the ball can actually land in 37 or 38 of them.

The edge, in plain arithmetic

Take a straight-up bet on a single number. On the European wheel, the true odds are 36 to 1 — 36 losing pockets for every winning one — but the casino pays 35 to 1. That missing unit is the house edge: one pocket out of 37, or 1 divided by 37, which is 2.70%. For every $100 you bet, you lose about $2.70 on average.

On the American wheel, the true odds are 37 to 1 — 37 losing pockets for every winner — and the payout is still 35 to 1. Now two units are missing instead of one: the edge is 2 divided by 38, or 5.26%. For every $100 wagered, you lose about $5.26 on average. The second zero adds a whole extra losing pocket while the payout stays frozen at 35 to 1.

The same math applies to every bet on the layout. Red or black looks like a coin flip, but it is not: on the European wheel, 18 red pockets out of 37 win, which is 48.6%, and on the American wheel, 18 out of 38 win, which is 47.4%. Dozens, columns, splits, corners — all carry exactly the same 2.70% or 5.26% edge, because every payout is priced off a 36-pocket wheel that does not exist. There is no "safer" bet on a roulette table.

One exception: some European tables offer "la partage" or "en prison" on even-money bets. Under la partage you lose only half your bet on zero; under en prison it is held for the next spin. Either rule cuts the edge on those bets to about 1.35%.

Why no betting system changes the edge

Roulette has attracted more betting systems than any other casino game, and none of them works. The most famous is the Martingale: bet on red, and every time you lose, double your bet, so the first win recovers all your losses plus a small profit. But losing streaks run longer than intuition suggests — eight losses in a row is uncommon but entirely ordinary over an evening, and by the eighth bet you are staking 128 times your original bet to win one unit. And the table limit cuts you off: most tables cap bets at 100 or 200 times the minimum, so a streak of seven or eight losses leaves you unable to place the next doubled bet.

Every other system fails for the same reason. Fibonacci, Labouchère, D'Alembert, betting on "hot" numbers or against "cold" ones — they all rearrange the order and size of your bets, and none changes the edge on a single dollar wagered. Each spin is independent: the wheel has no memory, and a number that has not hit in an hour is exactly as likely to hit next as the number that just hit twice.

What this means in practice

If both wheels are available, play the European one. Over an evening of $2,000 wagered, the European wheel costs you about $54 on average, while the American wheel costs you about $105. Same game, same bets, same entertainment — half the price. On cruise ships and in many American casinos, the European wheel is harder to find, sometimes at higher minimums. That is exactly the point: the house knows which wheel is cheaper for you.

When you do play, treat the edge as the price of admission and size your play accordingly. Roulette spins fast — 40 or more spins an hour is normal — so even modest bets add up to a large amount wagered over a session. At $10 a spin on a European wheel, an hour of play is roughly $400 wagered and an expected loss of about $11. On the American wheel, the same hour costs about $21. The difference between them is the only choice at the table that matters.
